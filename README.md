## <span style="color: #028090; font-weight: 700;">What is Pediatric Cancer?</span>

Pediatric cancer refers to cancers that occur in children and teens under the age of 20. Unlike adult cancers, which are often linked to lifestyle or environmental factors, childhood cancers are largely the result of DNA changes in cells that take place very early in life.

The **three most common variants** of childhood cancers include:
* <span style="font-weight: 800; color: #028090;">Leukemia</span> (blood cancers)
* <span style="font-weight: 800; color: #028090;">Brain and central nervous system tumors</span>
* <span style="font-weight: 800; color: #028090;">Lymphomas</span> (cancers of the immune system)

## <span style="color: #028090; font-weight: 700;">How You Can Make a Difference</span>

Beating childhood cancer requires global awareness, funding for targeted medical research, and community support for affected families. Because pediatric cancers behave differently than adult cancers, they require unique, specialized treatments.

You can get involved directly by <span style="font-weight: 800; color: #028090;">educating your local community</span>, participating in <span style="font-weight: 800; color: #028090;">blood drives</span>, or supporting verified pediatric oncology research foundations.
